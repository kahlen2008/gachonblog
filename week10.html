academics & project work

after surviving last week's gruelling pen-and-paper operating systems midterm, the moment of truth arrived this week: getting our exam papers back! to uphold academic integrity across such a large cohort, our professor had distributed multiple distinct versions of the midterm during exam day. when handing the graded papers back, he called students up systematically by the exact exam version they took. sitting in the lecture hall watching everyone collect their papers was pure suspense. flipping through my paper, seeing the professor's red annotations, and realising that all the late-night revision on CPU scheduling, memory paging, and process concurrency had paid off was an enormous relief!

collecting OS results 📝
sets & functions 📐

over in discrete mathematics, we transitioned from propositional logic truth tables and circuit diagrams straight into the deep waters of set theory and functions! we spent the week exploring set operations—unions, intersections, complements, power sets, and cartesian products—before diving into the mathematical definitions of functions. we analysed what makes a function injective (one-to-one), surjective (onto), or bijective (one-to-one correspondence), along with function composition and inverse mappings. it's fascinating seeing how these abstract set-theoretic concepts form the structural backbone of computer science—like how relational database queries map directly onto cartesian products and set differences, and how bijective functions are fundamental to cryptographic hashing and data encoding!

meanwhile, we kicked off our major web programming group project! the project is built using fundamental web standards—HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript. one of the greatest privileges of studying on exchange here at gachon is working alongside international classmates from diverse backgrounds, including wonderful teammates from nepal. collaborating together has been so rewarding, sharing different coding habits and brainstorming UI ideas. in fact, that multicultural teamwork sparked an inspiring feature idea: implementing a multi-language switcher on our website! by structuring localised language dictionaries with dynamic JS rendering, visitors from different countries can easily browse our site in their preferred language. bringing ideas born from our global friendship into real code feels so fulfilling!

web project with the team 💻
google lighthouse audit 💡

alongside our group project kickoff, this week's lectures introduced us to an indispensable developer tool: google lighthouse! integrated directly into chrome devtools, lighthouse runs automated audits on any webpage and grades it across four foundational pillars: performance (tracking page load speeds, resource weight, and core web vitals like LCP and layout shift), accessibility (verifying that screen readers, keyboard navigation, colour contrast ratios, and semantic HTML make the site usable for everyone), best practices (checking for HTTPS security, modern web APIs, and clean console logs), and SEO (ensuring search engines can crawl, index, and surface content with proper metadata). getting that instant 0–100 score complete with actionable diagnostics showed us that great frontend engineering isn't just about visual design, but about engineering fast, inclusive, and accessible web experiences for all users!

campus life & reflections

evening shuttle savior! 🚌

gachon's steep hills are notorious, and by evening our legs certainly feel every step. usually, our campus shuttle service (the famous red "ladybug" bus) wraps up around 5:30pm. but on particularly busy evenings when classes or group project meetings run late, the university surprises us by extending the shuttle service until 6:00pm or even 6:30pm! even better, instead of the smaller red minibuses, the school rolls out a huge white charter bus with double the passenger capacity. stepping out into the brisk evening chill and finding that warm, spacious bus waiting at the stop to whisk us straight up the mountain to our dorms felt like an absolute blessing.

standing on the campus vantage terrace and gazing out across gachon university, week 10 brings a quiet moment of deep reflection. we are now officially more than halfway through our exchange journey and the university semester. it feels like only yesterday that we landed at incheon with bulging luggage, struggling with basic transit cards, feeling lost on campus, and nervously deciphering korean menus. now, gachon feels like second nature—we know the shortcut paths between buildings, the local eateries, and the subway lines by heart. seeing the campus hills ablaze with rich autumn red and gold is breathtaking, but there's also a bittersweet twinge realising just how quickly the days are flying past. it's a poignant reminder to savour every moment before the semester draws to a close.

halfway mark view 🍂

anseong farmland

anseong farmland 🌾

for our weekend adventure, we ventured south into the gyeonggi countryside to visit anseong farmland (안성팜랜드)! the farm is located quite far from campus in anseong city—navigating multiple local buses would have taken ages, so we hailed a kakao taxi (kakao t). even in a direct cab cruising along the expressway, it still took over an hour to get there! what made this trip so special was going with my godmother and her child, who are part korean and happened to be visiting korea for a holiday at the exact same time. catching up with family while exploring the countryside was wonderful, chatting about our exchange life during the long cab ride. as soon as we arrived, the vast open pastures, rustic wooden fences, and sweeping views of grazing horses immediately made the long journey feel completely worth it!

just like misa gyeongjeong park from week 9, anseong farmland also features expansive fields of gorgeous pink muhly grass! but while misa park's pink muhly lined flat lakeside promenades, anseong farmland's pink muhly blankets wide, undulating rolling hills. walking through those sweeping pastel-pink plumes with traditional windmills spinning softly on the horizon under the autumn sky felt like wandering through a dreamscape. the contrast of fluffy pink grass against the clear blue sky made for endless beautiful family photos with my godmother and her kid! we spent ages strolling along the winding dirt paths, admiring how the soft pink sea shimmered in the afternoon sunshine and feeling the crisp autumn breeze blow gently across the hilltops.

pink muhly hills 🌸
friendly alpaca 🦙

beyond the scenic flowers, anseong farmland is home to dozens of wonderful animals you can interact with up close! you can buy little cups of animal feed and carrots to feed the horses, sheep, goats, and alpacas. these alpacas were ridiculously charming—with their fuzzy topknots, big soulful eyes, and gentle temperaments, they would curiously stretch their long necks over the fence to nibble pellets right from our hands. godmother's kid was totally mesmerised, and seeing how well-cared-for and content the animals looked made the visit so heartwarming.

one of our absolute favourite stops of the day was the dedicated rabbit village, where visitors actually get to interact with and feed dozens of free-roaming bunnies up close! right at the entrance, you can grab special feeding ladles loaded with fresh green grass, designed so that both kids and adults can feed the rabbits safely without accidentally getting their fingers nibbled. as soon as you step inside and lower the scoop near the ground, the bunnies immediately perk up, twitching their little noses and curious whiskers before eagerly hopping right toward you! this sweet little bunny was sitting peacefully in a mound of soft wood shavings, taking tiny, enthusiastic bites of fresh green grass directly from our ladle. it was so calm, friendly, and tame that it happily let us gently stroke its silky, velvety fur while it munched away—watching those tiny front paws resting on the wood shavings while it happily munched on the grass was pure cuteness overload that melted all our hearts!

feeding the bunny 🐰
bunny cuddle puddle 🌾🐇

just a few steps over in the communal pen, an entire colony of fluffy rabbits had gathered together in the most irresistible, cosy "cuddle puddle" imaginable! there were bunnies of every coat and colour—pure white, spotted, chocolate brown, and sandy grey—all huddled tightly shoulder-to-shoulder to stay warm against the brisk autumn countryside wind. when we brought over our feeding ladles filled with bundles of fresh green grass, the whole squad sprang into action! a dozen eager little heads crowded around the ladle in harmony, their floppy ears bobbing up and down as they took turns nibbling furiously. godmother's child was having the absolute time of their life holding the ladle, laughing with pure joy as the fluffy crew scrambled to get their fair share of fresh grass. between the gentle petting, the non-stop feeding, and the happy chatter of families around the enclosure, the rabbit village was easily one of the most delightful, heartwarming highlights of our entire trip down to anseong!

comfort eats & treats

for lunch during our outing, my godmother recommended that we try authentic korean beef tripe soup (내장탕 - naejangtang)! having a part-korean family member introduce us to local delicacies is the best way to discover dishes that exchange students might otherwise overlook. the soup arrived piping hot inside a heavy, bubbling stone ddukbaegi, packed with tender beef, hearty beef tripe, bean sprouts, and scallions in a deep, fiery-red broth. it definitely packed a spicy punch that warmed us up instantly, but the depth of rich, slow-simmered beef flavour was incredible. scooping steaming broth over white rice after a breezy morning outside was pure culinary comfort!

beef tripe soup 🍲
free "서비스" granola yoajung 🍨

to satisfy our sweet tooth, we naturally had to order yoajung (요아정) again! this time, i chose rich cheesecake cubes to layer over the signature hard chocolate shells. but when we opened the container, we were surprised to find a generous mound of golden crunchy granola sprinkled on top, completely free of charge! my godmother's husband explained that this is an iconic piece of korean culture: known as "service" (서비스 - seobiseu), where shop owners and staff gift complimentary items or toppings as an expression of hospitality and gratitude to their patrons. the sweet, nutty crunch of the bonus granola paired impeccably with the tart frozen yoghurt and velvety cheese bites—making this sweet gesture the sweetest ending to week 10!
