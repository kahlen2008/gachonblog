academics & schedule

after all the intense post-midterm hustle, week 11 started on an unexpectedly joyous note in operating systems! during our monday session, the entire class focused diligently on powering through our lab implementations. because everyone was locked in and wrapped up the required lab tasks ahead of schedule, our professor decided we had earned a well-deserved breather and cancelled our scheduled 1-hour tuesday class! getting that sudden notification saying class was off felt like Christmas morning — having an extra free hour in the morning to sleep in and catch up on personal errands was the ultimate perk of staying on top of our lab work!

free tuesday break! 🎉
db group assignment 📊

on the other hand, the pressure is steadily ramping up for our database systems coursework as the final assignment deadline creeps closer! our group is an awesome multicultural mix — i'm teaming up with coursemates from lithuania, who are fellow exchange students just like me, and students from vietnam, who are full-time international degree students studying here in korea. to ensure we could hit the ground running with realistic enterprise schemas rather than starting entirely from scratch, our professor kindly gave us permission to adapt existing database projects from our home universities. after evaluating our options, we decided to build upon our lithuanian teammates' database architecture! the core schema and entity relationships are rock solid; our main task right now is systematically translating all the original lithuanian attribute names, constraints, and table documentation over to english. it's such a unique collaborative experience bridging technical terminology across different languages!

meanwhile in web programming, our timetable underwent another very welcome, student-friendly tweak. our regular monday class is scheduled for just a single hour from 6:00pm to 7:00pm. for many international students who commute from off-campus dorms or have dinner plans, trekking all the way across campus for a 60-minute evening session was always a bit awkward. recognising this, our professor thoughtfully moved our monday evening lecture online via zoom! attending the lecture right from the comfort of our desks made the start of the week so much smoother and far more convenient for everyone involved.

online web prog zoom 💻

eom yong baek in jonggak

eom yong baek (종각) 🥢

for our big foodie outing this week, we headed straight into jongno to visit eom yong baek (엄용백돼지국밥) at their famous jonggak branch! the jongno 3-ga / jonggak neighbourhood was actually ranked by time out as the #1 coolest neighbourhood in the entire world, thanks to its vibrant maze of historic alleys, traditional hanok eateries, and buzzing street-food pocha culture. eom yong baek is legendary across south korea for its authentic busan-style pork soup rice (dwaeji gukbap). back home in singapore along boon tat street in the CBD, the brand is widely known as um yong baek and commands massive daily queues — so getting to visit their celebrated flagship branch right here in central seoul was an absolute must!

the menu offers several distinct variations of their celebrated pork soup rice — ranging from the clear, light broth to the deep, rich broth, covering both authentic busan style and richer milyang style. we went for the rich pork soup rice (밀양식 극상 돼지국밥), priced at 14,000 won. this is substantially cheaper than back in singapore, where a single bowl easily costs around $22 SGD! the bowl was brimming with premium, melt-in-your-mouth cuts of pork, spring onions, and perfectly soaked rice. interestingly, comparing the two, the soup broth in singapore felt noticeably stronger and more concentrated in flavour, whereas the seoul original was much cleaner, lighter, and more naturally refined — delivering a soothing, comforting warmth without any heaviness.

rich pork soup rice (14,000₩) 🍲
badger hat (8,000₩) 🥣

alongside our gukbap, we couldn't resist ordering their famous boiled pork maw (오소리감투 - osori-gamtu), hilariously translated on menus as "badger hat"! in korean folklore, pork stomach was historically so tender and delicious that kitchen workers would secretly hide it away as quickly as a badger slipping into its burrow, giving rise to its quirky nickname. it only cost 8,000 won here — compared to about $13 SGD in singapore! the pork maw was sliced into delicate, chewy ribbons that had zero gaminess and a wonderfully springy bite. it came accompanied by a side of bright red dipping sauce: while i initially braced myself for a fiery spicy kick, it turned out to be a pleasantly sweet, tangy seasoned sauce (chogochujang style) that paired brilliantly with the savoury pork!

deoksugung & city hall

additionally, my godmother had warmly recommended taking an afternoon walk around city hall to soak in central seoul's rich architectural contrasts. standing right at the bustling seoul plaza intersection, the vista is stunning: directly ahead looms the futuristic, wave-shaped glass facade of the new seoul city hall, set right behind the dignified 1925 stone building of the seoul metropolitan library (the historic former city hall), with the towering seoul sinmun (서울신문) building standing prominently to the left. if you turn your gaze just slightly to the left from here, the grand historic gates and picturesque stone-wall pathways of deoksugung palace unfold right before your very eyes!

seoul plaza intersection 🏙️
daehanmun gate guard ceremony 💂‍♂️

just steps from the intersection stands daehanmun gate (대한문), the grand main entrance to deoksugung palace! regular adult admission to the palace is usually 1,000 won, but to our pleasant surprise, entry was completely free on the day we visited! it turned out to coincide with the nationwide autumn tourism campaign where royal palaces waive admission fees for the public to enjoy the peak autumn foliage. we arrived at the perfect time to witness the famous royal guard changing ceremony outside the gate. this colourful, ceremonial joseon dynasty reenactment takes place twice daily at 11:00am and 2:00pm (except mondays and during adverse weather). what's especially great for visitors is that they provide running announcements in both korean and english over the speakers throughout the event, so foreign tourists can easily follow along and understand exactly what is happening during each phase. watching the guards in their vivid crimson and blue traditional uniforms, beating resonant ceremony drums and marching with ceremonial banners against the backdrop of modern downtown seoul, was an utterly captivating spectacle!

curving directly along the exterior wall of the palace is seoul's most romantic walkway: the iconic deoksugung doldam-gil (stone-wall road)! in autumn, this 900-metre pedestrian boulevard transforms into a golden paradise, with towering ginkgo trees showering the cobblestones with vibrant yellow leaves. if this picturesque pathway looks familiar, it's because it has starred as the filming location in iconic k-dramas like Goblin and Extraordinary Attorney Woo! interestingly, local urban legend warns that couples who stroll down this path together are destined to break up — a superstition born from the historical fact that the seoul family court was once located right at the end of the road!

autumn doldam-gil 🍁
seoul museum of art 🏛️

following the gentle curve of the stone-wall road up the hill leads directly into the peaceful tree-lined courtyard of the seoul museum of art (SeMA seosomun main branch). the building itself is a masterpiece of architectural heritage: its stately red-brick renaissance facade was originally constructed in 1928 as the supreme court of korea, now seamlessly integrated with a sleek, contemporary gallery space behind it. greeting visitors in the front courtyard is the famous vibrant outdoor sculpture titled La Vie en Rose (2012) by artist choi jeong-hwa, intricately crafted out of synthetic plastic materials into a massive blossoming bouquet. wandering around the sculpture-filled grounds and taking in the autumn colours beneath the ancient trees was the perfect cultural finale to our palace walk!

daily living & shopping

november in korea also means one thrilling event for skincare and beauty enthusiasts: the annual olive young black friday big sale! olive young is korea's premier health and beauty drugstore, and their black friday promotion is famous for slashing prices up to 60% or 70% across viral k-beauty skincare, sheet masks, and grooming essentials. stepping into the store felt like entering a buzzing festival, with eye-catching sale banners, limited-edition gift bundles, and shoppers eagerly filling their baskets with winter moisturizers and serums. it was practically impossible to walk out without grabbing a few skincare steals!

olive young black friday 🛍️
coin laundromat run 🧺

on the domestic front, i had a practical student life lesson this week: navigating neighbourhood coin laundromats! even though my godmother stays in a fully furnished apartment when in town, in-unit washing machines in korean flats are often compact washer-dryer combos that take hours to cycle and can't handle bulky autumn duvets and heavy winter outerwear. to get thick bedding and winter jackets washed and dried in one quick go, she took me over to a 24-hour self-service coin laundromat down the street! she showed me the ropes of using the change machines — slotting in 10,000-won notes in exchange for stacks of 500-won coins to feed into the high-capacity commercial washers and jumbo tumble dryers. watching everything spin while enjoying the warm smell of fabric softener made laundry day surprisingly therapeutic!

back around campus, we also made our regular pilgrimage to our trusty favourite eatery near gachon — hi dojo! while their classic katsudon is already fantastic, their cheese katsudon (9,000 won) has truly become our ultimate comfort benchmark. the dish arrives piping hot: a hefty, succulent pork cutlet fried to golden-brown perfection, simmered with sweet translucent onions and savory dashi-egg broth, all crowned under a thick, bubbling blanket of melted mozzarella cheese. taking that first bite gives an incredible, stretchy cheese pull that stretches almost a foot high! what elevates the whole meal is hi dojo's generous self-service bar, where you can help yourself to unlimited bowls of chilled, spicy kimchi and crisp pickled yellow radish. having that sharp, tangy, garlicky kimchi on hand to slice right through the creamy, cheesy tonkatsu makes every single mouthful delightfully balanced — truly feeling like a warm, hearty hug after a demanding day of classes.

cheese katsudon (9,000₩) 🧀
