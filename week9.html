midterms & exams

midterm season reached its absolute peak this week, starting bright and early at 9am with our operating systems (OS) midterm! sitting down with a thick, physical paper exam was quite an intense wake-up call. by the end of the exam paper, my writing hand was completely cramped and aching from non-stop scribbling! this was especially true for the long-answer questions — our professor gave us an entire full A4 sheet size of blank space just to write down our thorough explanations for a single question. having to write out detailed operating system concepts, process scheduling diagrams, and architectural workflows entirely by hand with zero backspace or undo button required serious mental endurance and careful planning before setting pen to paper.

9am OS midterm 📝
handwritten code! 🤖

if OS tested our handwriting stamina, the machine learning (ML) exam took the crown as the undisputed final boss of midterms! the single biggest shock was having to write actual code by hand on paper. back at NYP in singapore, we always write code inside VSCode — so handwriting implementations for machine learning models line-by-line onto physical paper felt totally unnatural and tricky. on top of that, the exam format was pure adrenaline: we had 1 theory question and 2 coding questions that had to be completed in a razor-thin window of just 10 minutes! immediately after the timer ran out, we had to explain our code and logic to our professor on the spot. before classes started this semester, i honestly assumed OS would be the hardest subject, but seeing how ML conducted its exams completely flipped my expectations — this was definitely the toughest hurdle yet!

alongside the coding prep, our ML revision schedule had everyone closely monitoring their exam slots and reviewing core concepts across linear and logistic regression, SVM, and KNN models. knowing that you only have 10 quick minutes to produce clean code and defend your thought process verbally meant there was zero room for second-guessing. we spent hours running through algorithm logic, formula derivations, and practising explaining our solutions out loud so we wouldn't freeze up in front of the professor. it was nerve-wracking, but surviving that intense session gave all of us such a huge sigh of relief!

10-minute ML sprint ⏱️
discrete math revision 📐

meanwhile in discrete mathematics, our professor assigned us homework on logical propositions, though it was honestly structured as crucial revision for our upcoming midterms. the stakes for this paper are exceptionally high because math midterms carry a massive 40% weightage of our final grade! just like our other papers, this exam was held strictly on paper — very different from our experience at NYP, where modules like applied math in computing and statistical research methods were conducted as online exams. discrete math tested us thoroughly on constructing massive truth tables and solving circuitorial propositions (where you have to trace inputs through complex logic gate circuits — AND, OR, NOT, XOR — and derive the exact output boolean expression). getting through all those gate combinations without flipping a single 1 or 0 required laser focus.

fortunately, web programming was an absolute breath of fresh air amidst the written exam chaos! it was the only midterm exam conducted online, and the entire paper consisted of multiple-choice questions (MCQs). our professor had kindly assured us beforehand that almost all our midterm questions would be drawn directly from the weekly quiz questions we had already completed throughout the semester. as long as you diligently reviewed your previous quizzes and understood the concepts, the exam felt smooth, predictable, and totally stress-free. it was such a welcome relief compared to writing code on paper!

online web prog MCQs 🌐
os lab assignments 💻

following the completion of midterms, our OS professor announced an exciting shift in our curriculum: replacing smaller theoretical quizzes with more hands-on lab assignments! rather than just memorising definitions about memory paging, process scheduling, and thread synchronisation for quick multiple-choice checks, working through guided lab assignments lets us actually implement and observe operating system mechanisms firsthand. it takes a bit more time to debug, but learning by doing is so much more rewarding and directly connects with real software engineering!

pink muhly at misa gyeongjeong park

misa speedboat park 🏞️

with our intense midterms finally behind us, we celebrated by heading out on an autumn day trip to misa gyeongjeong park (미사경정공원, commonly known as misa speedboat park) located over in hanam! the park was originally constructed as the official canoeing and rowing regatta venue for the 1988 seoul olympics, featuring a massive, crystal-clear 2-kilometre-long lake surrounded by lush green lawns and scenic walking trails. it took us about an hour by public transit from gachon, but getting away from campus to breathe the crisp autumn air was the ultimate mental reset.

the main highlight of visiting misa park during autumn is its famous pink muhly grass, which blooms into gorgeous fluffy pastel clouds from late september right through early november! interestingly, we learned that finding pink muhly inside seoul has become surprisingly difficult recently: several famous spots within the city (such as haneul park) have cleared or phased out their fields because the ministry of environment designated pink muhly as a "species of ecological concern" back in 2019 due to fears that this hardy foreign grass might invade and outcompete native korean plants. however, misa gyeongjeong park in gyeonggi-do still lovingly maintains expansive, designated beds of pink muhly alongside walking paths where it can be enjoyed safely without spreading uncontrollably!

pink muhly sea 🌸
autumn serenity 🍂

standing right in the middle of those soft pink plumes swaying gently under the warm autumn sunshine felt like stepping straight into a fairytale. the contrast between the dreamy pink flowerbeds, the golden autumn trees lining the paths, and the sprawling blue waters of the regatta lake was absolutely breathtaking. it was so peaceful to just slow down for an afternoon after such a hectic week. despite being an hour away from school by subway and bus, walking along the park paths, enjoying the cool breeze, and snapping photos with friends was easily one of our favourite weekend memories of the semester so far!

comfort eats & treats

inside isaac toast 🥪

we also popped by our local isaac toast outlet for breakfast! the shop is small and welcoming, with an easy-to-use ordering kiosk right by the door that features a full english language option — making ordering completely seamless. inside, there are a few counter seats where you can rest your feet and watch how everything is made while waiting for your order. the aunties running the shop are genuinely some of the sweetest, friendliest people you'll meet! watching them generously butter the thick bread slices on the hot flat-top grill and assemble each toast with fresh eggs and fillings was so comforting.

this week we ordered a proper feast: the deep cheese bacon potato toast (5,500 won), the scrambled bacon toast (5,100 won), and a side of potato pops (2,200 won). both toasts were out of this world — crispy, golden buttery bread grilled to perfection, paired with savoury bacon, gooey melted cheese, fluffy eggs, and isaac's signature sweet kiwi-honey spread that makes korean street toast so legendary. the potato pops were bite-sized, crunchy, and seasoned nicely, though the portion was definitely a little small for 2,200 won. while i probably wouldn't get the potato pops again, the hearty toasts themselves are an absolute comfort staple that we will definitely keep coming back for!

buttery toast feast ✨
honeycomb yoajung! 🍯

since honeycomb was sold out the last time we tried yoajung, we decided to give it another shot this week. after an exhausting day of midterms and chilly weather outside, we didn't really feel like walking out to get dessert, so we just ordered it straight to our dorm through baemin. we finally managed to get the one with the honeycomb chunk, and it honestly paired really well with the frozen yoghurt since the tartness made the honey taste a lot less overwhelming. that being said, adding the honeycomb brought the total to over 10,000 won, which is honestly pretty expensive. it was definitely nice to try once, but if i order from yoajung again, i'll probably skip the honeycomb and stick to regular toppings to save some money!

and of course, nothing grounds a busy week like a comforting home-cooked meal in the dorm. after a quick grocery run down to the e-mart right outside school, we gathered thinly sliced beef, fresh chye sim greens, enoki mushrooms, and hearty blocks of tofu to cook a steaming, fragrant hotpot together. sitting around the table in our room, watching the broth gently bubble away and sharing piping-hot soup packed with wholesome veggies gave us that warm feeling of home. after relying so much on takeout, cafe food, and convenience store bites during exam prep, having a clean, nourishing meal was the perfect boost to recharge our bodies and wrap up a chaotic midterm grind on a comforting note.
