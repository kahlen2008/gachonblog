overview

week 1 was a mix of excitement, nervousness and countless firsts. from saying goodbye to our families in singapore, to finally stepping onto korean soil, everything felt unreal. it's the start of a new chapter, and we're all so grateful to be here.

arrival in seoul

we landed at incheon international airport in the evening. the airport was huge and so well-organised — everything from immigration to baggage claim was super smooth. the cool weather immediately felt different from singapore, and i remember being so excited just seeing korean signboards everywhere. to get to the university, i took the call van service that gachon provided directly from the airport! the drive was super comfortable, and the views along the way were absolutely stunning, especially getting to see the beautiful night skyline for the first time.

settling in

my cozy dorm bed ♡

i moved into the gachon university dormitory on the same day. the room is simple but incredibly cozy—it has absolutely everything i need for the next few months. unpacking felt a little overwhelming at first, but once i laid out my soft blanket and added my favorite plushie, the space instantly transformed into a comforting little haven. after setting up, i went for a short walk around campus to get familiar with the area. it was already quite late, but the campus still felt lively and welcoming.

after unpacking and organising my little study desk, i felt so accomplished. having a dedicated space to study and journal makes the dorm feel much more like my own personal sanctuary. i brought some of my favorite stationery from home and bought a few new cute items from a local store to decorate it. it's perfectly set up for all the late-night study sessions to come!

all set up!
laundry day!

another extremely important part of settling in is our dorm's laundry room! doing laundry here is surprisingly cheap and easy—it only costs 1,000 won (about 1 sgd) to wash a load, and another 1 sgd to use the dryer. figuring out the korean buttons on the machines was a bit of a fun puzzle at first, but it's just another one of those little everyday things that makes living abroad feel extremely surreal!

first meals in korea

4 sgd carbonara! ♡

of course, we couldn't come to korea without diving straight into the local food! ironically, our very first meal wasn't at a fancy restaurant, but something straight out of a k-drama—convenience store food! i grabbed a bacon carbonara for just 4 sgd, and honestly, it was incredible. there is something so comforting about sitting in a quiet convenience store late at night, enjoying a quick, hot meal after a long day of flying and hauling luggage. it was cheap, surprisingly delicious, and the perfect cozy start to my culinary adventure here.

the next day, we had our school orientation and they treated us to our second meal in korea—a huge bowl of authentic bibimbap! we ate at the cafeteria located right inside gachon university's beautiful vision tower. since it was provided for free by the school, it tasted even better! mixing all the fresh vegetables, gochujang, and rice together with my new friends from orientation was such a fun experience.

free bibimbap!
army stew ♡

later that week, we had our first proper restaurant meal, and it was an absolute lifesaver. i tried buddae jiggae (army stew), and it was served bubbling hot right at the table. it was loaded with ramen noodles, spam, sausages, seafood, and cheese, all swimming in a perfectly spicy and savory broth. the rich, comforting flavors instantly washed away all my travel fatigue. there's really nothing quite like authentic korean comfort food to make you feel right at home!

the culinary adventures didn't stop there. a few days later, i had my first taste of real andong jjimdak (braised chicken). the dish was absolutely massive! it came packed with tender chunks of chicken, potatoes, carrots, and an abundance of chewy glass noodles, all soaked in this incredible sweet and savory soy sauce broth. the subtle kick of spice mixed with the sweetness was simply heavenly. i'm quickly learning that eating in korea is an event in itself—the portions are generous, the side dishes (banchan) are endlessly refillable, and every meal is something to look forward to.

jjimdak!

orientation & cafe hopping

the squad!

one of the highlights of the week was definitely our school orientation. we were split up into these totally random groups, which ended up being such a blessing because i got to meet so many amazing exchange students from all over the world! our group was incredibly diverse, and despite coming from completely different backgrounds, we instantly clicked. we spent the whole morning doing fun icebreakers and going on campus tours to figure out where our classes would be.

after an amazing campus tour, the school was sweet enough to treat all of us to free drinks! we headed over to ripple coffee, this super aesthetic cafe located right inside our campus. nothing bonds a group of confused, jet-lagged international students quite like a good iced coffee and figuring out korean university life together. it was the perfect way to wrap up the orientation and kick off our semester.

ripple coffee ☕
from all over the world 🌍

it's so fascinating hearing everyone's different stories and cultural backgrounds. even though we are far away from our respective homes, having this little international family here in korea makes the transition so much easier. i can't wait to see what the rest of the semester has in store for all of us!

shin ramyun pop-up

finally, we also made our way down to seongsu-dong to visit the shin ramyun bunsik the factory! this is a special antenna pop-up space launched by nongshim to celebrate the 40th anniversary of their iconic instant noodles. the first floor had an interactive experiential zone where we could check out all sorts of unique merchandise. but the absolute highlight was the second floor! they served exclusive custom recipes and export-only variations of shin ramyun. there was even a diy station where we could build our own personalized ramen packs. it was such a fun experience!

nongshim pop-up!

what we learned

  • ✔ step out of our comfort zone
  • ✔ it's okay to feel nervous — it's part of the journey
  • ✔ small moments (like a good meal) can make a big difference
  • ✔ we're capable of adapting to new environments

that's a wrap for week 1! stay tuned for week 2 where we finally start our classes and experience real korean university life... ♡
